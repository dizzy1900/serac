# Coverage audit, 2026-09-04 — findings not yet closed

Both briefs (`serac-prompt-1-foundations.md`, `serac-prompt-2-modeling-core.md`) were audited
area by area against the tree, and every finding was independently re-verified before landing
here. Of 43 confirmed findings, **6 were closed in commit `f592b28`** and 1 more was resolved
by that same commit from a different angle. The **36 below are open**.

Read this alongside `RELEASE_STATUS.md`. Nothing here is an honest negative result — those are
recorded in the ledger and are not defects. These are places where the repository does less
than a brief asks, or says more than the code supports.

`OVERSTATED_CLAIM` is the most serious category in a project whose whole premise is that its
reports can be trusted: it means a document, model card or ledger entry asserts something the
tree does not support. Fix those before the merely missing work.

## Verdict from the audit

> Both briefs are implemented to a high degree and the tree's honest-negative machinery (refusals, criterion_unmet, null-not-guess) genuinely works; every model result the auditors questioned is real code that ran.
> The gaps that survive are almost entirely in the claims layer — RELEASE_STATUS.md, the model cards, ARCHITECTURE/DATA_SOURCES and code docstrings describe a tree that is in several places older, newer or different from the one on disk (no Dockerfile, no DVC lock, an unregistered `serac watch` CLI, an M2 latency table with no committed artefact, an M3 sweep the reports say "could not be computed") — plus one brief requirement nobody flagged: `make promote` has no `PROMOTE_APPROVED_BY` human gate at all.
